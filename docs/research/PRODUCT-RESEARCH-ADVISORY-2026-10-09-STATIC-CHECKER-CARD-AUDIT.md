**Purpose:** Determine how existing deterministic tools overlap all six Hapsland landing-page examples.
**Audience:** Product owners and contributors, including coding agents.
**Status:** Completed temporary advisory research; six dedicated agents plus follow-up discovery and replacement-example execution passes.

## Research brief and change log

Date: 2026-10-09 local / 2026-10-10 UTC. The original examples were captured from COMMENTS in [content.ts](../../packages/agent-flow-viz/site/content.ts), which now contains the selected replacement candidates. The original execution fixtures retain the frozen baseline; the report does not maintain a second copy of their code. Scope also includes the user's `field?: null | 'a'` example.

Existing-solution baseline: use existing deterministic tooling first. A finding on the original code, a correct invariant recommendation, safe automatic repair, and enforcement after a supplied invariant are separate capabilities. Broad bans count as overlap even when they do not selectively identify the intended business mistake. Unused-name and formatting diagnostics do not count as finding the highlighted concern.

Initial pass ran all originals, followed by two independent verification agents. The owner then requested one separate agent for EACH card and broader ecosystem discovery. Six dedicated agents ran independent fixtures, and this report supersedes the initial narrow coverage conclusions. Particularly, the original “two direct overlaps” characterization was too narrow: ready optional/null policies also warn on cards 1 and 3, laint recommends the displayed repair for card 4, and Callograph detects card 6 without a user-authored API blacklist, and Haskellish Effect's recommended preset rejects it with a scope-aware global/capability policy.

Discovery used official rule catalogs, npm/PyPI registries, released package source, first-party repositories/tests, TypeScript documentation, Semgrep public packs, CodeQL query help, DeepSource and Snyk catalogs. Queries included optional/null/undefined, dependent/jointly required fields, discriminated unions, nominal/branded IDs, primitive obsession, count/nonnegative/integer/refinement predicates, purity/effect/clock/global dependencies. Inclusion required plausible behavior on the shown concern; sorting-only, unrelated framework, security-only, runtime-only and other-source-language tools were excluded unless a relevant rule or alternative contract was identified. Source-only exclusions and unavailable tools remain explicit below.

This is a rule/checker comparison, not an agent-host architecture evaluation. Offline diagnostics can feed advisory or blocking workflows, but host enforcement, headless delivery, coexistence, timeout/crash policy, remote Jev failure and Hapsland detection accuracy were not tested. No live Jev or hosted source submission was made.

Stopping condition: after executing plausible discovered candidates, two focused discovery rounds without materially new applicable behavior in the declared channels. This is bounded search saturation, not enumeration of every public, private, historical or hosted tool. Root registry top-100 search pages are ranked discovery aids, not a complete package census. A ready precise diagnostic accepting our good controls would overturn the remaining selective-gap conclusions.

## Synthesis, disconfirmation, and handoff

The expanded pass overturns using these cards as evidence that ordinary deterministic tooling cannot notice the shown code. Every card has some overlap: ready broad policies, configured naming/API restrictions, or stronger related domain heuristics. Cards4/6 have particularly direct existing-tool alternatives. Exact selective discovery for cards1/2/3/5 remains unestablished in tested tools, but cheap project-specific policies and contextual rule scopes are serious baselines.

The defensible candidate distinction is choosing an appropriate invariant in context and proposing a model change across consumers; static tooling is already effective at enforcing supplied invariants. This research does not show that Hapsland actually performs that distinction accurately. A ready precise domain rule accepting our controls, or comparative Hapsland/lint fixture results, could change the recommendation.
