# Tower mechanisms for teaching Hapsland

**Purpose:** Rank the seven inspected pre-migration tower mechanisms as candidates for an exploratory balance laboratory.
**Status:** Temporary pre-migration source-inspection report; the owner selected the first three mechanisms for initial experiments on 2026-10-02. The final lineup remains open.
**Authority:** Design advice based on implementation inspection; accepted product contracts and issues #176/#199 own architecture and migration scope. This report does not establish human learning or empirical balance.
**Expected use:** Choose two or three provisional interventions and paired cases for the laboratory specification.
**Lifecycle:** When the initial mechanism cases are accepted at completion of [laboratory issue #203](https://github.com/dearlordylord/hapsland/issues/203), **consolidate** useful decisions and limitations into the optional-game guide and game design owner, update inbound links, and **delete** this report. Revisit before acceptance if #176/#199 change their simulation boundary.

## Recommendation

Start with **dependency-targeted acceleration (Coordinator), output batching (Packager), and launch pacing under fixed permits (Parallelizer)**. These name mechanisms, not permanent tower identities. Their current implementations can be replaced while keeping the teaching questions and observation requirements. Coordinator versus general acceleration gives a useful later comparison; Shield gives a useful negative control.

This is a priority for explaining actual Hapsland ownership and causality, not a strongest-tower ranking. It is inferred from code and contracts. No new runs, user study, visual review or measurement were performed for this report.

Owner decision after the assessment: use these first three mechanisms as the initial experimental set. This accepts the study selection, not the final abilities, names, balance or evidence of learning.

The source assessment below predates the continuous-consumer rewrite. Current capability availability is recorded in the [laboratory guide](../prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/README.md#current-ability-boundary-and-remaining-work); its three provisional business interventions are unsupported today.

## Priority

| Priority | Current tower | Teaching value and causal intervention | Risk / qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Coordinator | Targets the still-running Jev sibling of already retained findings. Makes dependency waiting and shared family eligibility tangible. Accelerating the blocker can change when retained work becomes collectable. | The current fixture waits for its finite workload before output; eligibility becoming true need not immediately cause delivery. The new continuous consumer must expose the actual causal chain instead of carrying over that fixture policy. |
| 2 | Packager | Changes future batch target and modeled serialization time while committed membership, bytes, authorization and ACK remain distinct. Shows why writer completion is not retirement. | Current target and serialization policy are synthetic. A tower must not bypass real collection, freshness or output authority. |
| 3 | Parallelizer | Changes host launch pacing from a baseline below the fixed pool ceiling. Demonstrates admission versus service speed and shared-resource contention. | Does not increase production capacity. The existing name can misleadingly imply a production configurable worker count; label the intervention as simulated launch pacing and preserve real limits. |
| 4 | Rapid | Changes travel/service effort and earlier modeled callbacks; useful baseline against Coordinator and Parallelizer. | Road distance and service duration are game/environment assumptions. Faster arrival cannot create a permit, bypass eligibility or acknowledge output. |
| 5 | Refiner | Demonstrates prevention before an outcome is observed, and the inability to erase an already retained finding. Has a clear upstream/downstream effect. | It lowers synthetic outcome risk. Hapsland does not improve source correctness by lowering a review probability. A future replacement may model actual simulated repair behavior; that is a separate design decision. |
| 6 | Shield | Excellent control: changes health loss while ownership/bytes/queue remain. Shows distinction between presentation damage and business progress. | It teaches a game boundary more than a real Hapsland mechanism. Never represent shield health protection as capacity release or production reliability. |
| 7 | Relay | Makes coverage versus strength visible, with the same kind of intervention as Rapid. | Primarily spatial/game design; little additional architectural teaching until placement is itself the research question. |

## Paired cases to build first

1. **Dependency waiting:** hold workload, outcomes, seed and placement scope fixed. Before: one child has a retained finding while another is still reviewing. After targeted acceleration: blocker settles earlier and record the time eligibility changes, then collection and ACK separately. Compare acceleration of unrelated work as a control. This does not prove universal progress, faster native Jev or immediate delivery.
2. **Committed output:** begin with a committed four-member batch; change batching/serialization policy while it runs. Existing members, byte charges and remaining writer duration stay fixed; only a subsequent batch changes. Separately delay ACK: ownership persists despite completed writer work. This does not show configurable production batching, receipt by an agent or advice adoption.
3. **Admission under saturation:** continuously offer work, hold actual pool limits fixed, compare two versus more simultaneous host launches below the same limit. Record admitted/active/waiting counts and downstream retention. Repeat with slow output so increased upstream throughput may worsen pressure. This does not prove higher production capacity, fairness or a globally beneficial upgrade.

Use health and completion only as secondary game outcomes. Teaching evidence needs the affected business observations and an explanation of unchanged boundaries. A finite seeded pair demonstrates that pair; it is not a balance theorem. Before/after screenshots can support a later design review only if the exact changed panel and causal behavior are identified.

## Architecture boundary and migration

The current native game calls production Canonical but owns a separate environment driver. Current Monkey Business exposes create/step/advance/subscription/replay and already simulates compiled policy and import-graph behavior. The accepted shared-engine direction in [#176](https://github.com/dearlordylord/hapsland/issues/176) and optional-game slice [#199](https://github.com/dearlordylord/hapsland/issues/199) require continuous workload, generic simulated-environment actions, and removal of authored waves and redundant game-side simulation. Geometry, combat, towers and economy remain game-owned. The dashboard and game use independent engine instances.

Therefore do not specify waves as the laboratory foundation, freeze these tower implementations, or extend DefenseHost into a second lasting engine. Use bounded continuous-workload windows and scripted interventions through the shared public boundary as it becomes available. #176 excludes balance overhaul; the laboratory is separately scoped and must not silently widen its migration tasks. [#200](https://github.com/dearlordylord/hapsland/issues/200) requires verification with the game absent and distinguishes production functions, modeled orchestration and unexercised native boundaries.
